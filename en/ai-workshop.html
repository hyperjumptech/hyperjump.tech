The starting point of this workshop

The rule of the room

Everyone types. Nobody only watches.

A briefing does not give you a position on AI. Using it on your own work does. Each person brings a laptop, opens the tools they already have, and works on files that look like their week. You will see AI do impressive things. You will also catch it failing. Both matter.

Who this is for

Teams that already use the tools, and now need a position

01

Leadership teams

You are asked to set direction on AI. You cannot do that from a vendor slide deck.

02

People who already live in ChatGPT or Gemini

Daily use is not the same as knowing when the answer is wrong, or when the tool should stay closed.

03

Organizations that will be held accountable

Letters, reports, and board papers still go out under your name. Someone has to check the work.

How it runs

Four sessions. Your work, not a curriculum from a binder.

We confirm dates with you. A short gap after the first day lets us turn what you told us into the hands-on exercises.

  1. Session 1

    25 min

    How these tools really work

    The basics, briefly. Then a calibration: what has changed, and where the tools still fail quietly. You leave knowing the three ways AI fails and how to spot each one.

  2. Session 2

    3 hours

    Discovery

    A conversation, not a lecture. We map weekly decisions, current AI use, and what must never go into a prompt. The output is a short list of exercises built from your actual work.

  3. Session 3

    3.5 hours

    Where it fails, and staying in charge

    On your own laptop, in small groups. Check sources. Turn a weekly task into instructions the tool can follow. Then decide, from what you just saw, what never goes in, who is accountable, and when AI is the wrong tool.

  4. Session 4

    3.5 hours

    Your work

    Apply it to tasks you are already doing this week. Draft, compare, prepare talking points. Write your own rules: when to use AI, how to verify, what never to paste, who signs off.

What you leave with

Understanding you can stand behind

The goal is not tool proficiency. It is a position you can hold when someone asks you what AI is good for, and what it is not.

01

You used it on your own work

Typed, tested, and caught at least one failure yourself. Not watched a demo.

02

Your own rules for using AI

When you use AI, how you verify, what never goes into a prompt, and who signs off before it leaves the building.

03

You can explain it without us in the room

What it is, what it does well, where it breaks, and what a responsible approach looks like. In your own words.
